# The hardware runs on record

Every run of this kit on a real GPU, whatever it showed. This page is written by `python results/record.py --write` from the bundles in this folder, and a test fails if it is out of date: apart from the first two columns, nothing in the table is typed in by hand. The account of each run, with what failed and why, is [docs/DEVIATIONS.md](../docs/DEVIATIONS.md).

All eight are shakedowns on Tesla T4s in cloud sessions, with the GPU's own NVML counters as the only meter. A T4 is not the target device and NVML can be spoofed by whoever runs the machine, so none of this is a claim about Plan A: it is what the pipeline and the pre-registered analysis did on the hardware that was available. The registered trial needs H100-class GPUs and an independent power meter ([docs/PREREGISTRATION.md](../docs/PREREGISTRATION.md)).

Reading the columns. **H1.1**: the detection threshold is valid (conformal, since amendment v0.6). **H1.2**: false alarms on blocks with no hidden load, at most 3% of at least 200. **H1.3**: hidden loads of at least 5% (primary) and 2% (stretch) of the GPU's capacity are detected at least 95% of the time in every class that has 8 or more such blocks; the count is over all classes. **H1.4**: the bound covers what the hidden load really used in at least 95% of hidden blocks. **Closure gap**: the smallest hidden share of capacity from which detection stays at 95% or more; **with slack** adds the efficiency slack the probe measured on that GPU, and the outcome needs that sum at 5% or less. Runs 3 to 5 used the straight-line analysis that run 5 showed to be wrong for a power-limited GPU; runs 6 to 8 are the first prospective tests of its replacement.

To check a row yourself: `closure verify` and `closure criteria --run` on the unpacked bundle, or `python results/reread.py results/<folder>` for everything the documents quote. [docs/VERIFY_IT_YOURSELF.md](../docs/VERIFY_IT_YOURSELF.md) has the commands.
